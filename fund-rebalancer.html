Portfolio rebalancer

Rebalancing tools for mutual fund, ETF, and stock portfolios. Works with Fidelity positions files.

This page has two tools. Pick the tab that matches the account you're rebalancing.

Mutual funds only, when every holding is a mutual fund from one fund family and they can be exchanged with each other without fees, such as Fidelity funds in an HSA or IRA. Rebalancing is done with exchanges, which sell one fund and buy another in a single order at the same day's closing price. It takes the fewest orders and leaves no cash waiting to settle.

Mixed portfolio, for everything else:

  • Accounts with ETFs or individual stocks, with or without mutual funds.
  • Accounts that hold only mutual funds, but from different fund families or with fees to exchange. Use this tab with Mutual funds here can be exchanged with each other turned off, and each fund is sold to cash or bought with cash on its own.

Each tab has its own holdings, targets, saved presets, and How this tool works guide.

Which tool should I use?

For a mix of ETFs, individual stocks, and mutual funds in one Fidelity account. Stocks and ETFs are traded in dollars (or in whole shares, for accounts without fractional trading); mutual funds are exchanged with each other where possible. Built with Fidelity in mind; the positions upload reads Fidelity's file only.

ETFs and stocks trade all day at changing prices, while mutual funds trade once a day at the closing price. So the plan comes in up to three steps:

  • Sell ETFs & stocks that are above target.
  • Mutual fund orders: exchanges between funds, plus any fund sales or purchases, placed before the market closes.
  • Buy ETFs & stocks that are below target, in dollars, so every dollar of cash is invested.

Before you start, download a fresh positions file during market hours so prices are current. After the sells, you can enter the cash you actually have and the final buys resize to match.

3. Options

  • Sell and buy: moves every position to its target. Positions above target are sold or exchanged, and the cash buys the ones below. Positions not in the target are sold completely.
  • Buy only with cash: never sells. It puts your cash (including new money) into the positions furthest below target, bringing them as close to target as the cash allows. Positions not in the target are left alone.

In a taxable account, Buy only avoids realizing capital gains, which makes it a good choice when you're adding money. Use Sell and buy when the portfolio has drifted too far for new cash to fix.

Skips funds that are already close to target, so you don't place tiny orders.

  • $: a fund within that many dollars of its target isn't traded.
  • %: a fund within that many percentage points of the total portfolio isn't traded. For example, 1% on a $10,000 portfolio means within $100.

The dollars a skipped fund would have moved go to the largest other buy instead, so cash still ends at your target. Funds being sold completely, and cash, are never skipped. Leave it at 0 for an exact rebalance.

Every order fills at today's closing price, but the plan has to use the last close, so results always land a little off target.

  • Shares (recommended): each exchange sells a set number of shares. If the market moves today, what you sell moves with it, which keeps your mix closer to target.
  • Dollars: each exchange moves a fixed dollar amount.

Either way, buys from cash are in dollars, and the final order out of a fund being sold completely sells all shares, so nothing is left behind.

Mutual funds

Turn this on when every mutual fund in the account is from the same fund family and can be exchanged without fees, such as all Fidelity funds.

  • On: the plan pairs mutual funds up, so a fund above target is exchanged straight into one below target. That means fewer orders and no waiting for a sale to settle.
  • Off: each mutual fund is sold to cash or bought with cash separately. Use this when the funds come from different families or an exchange would cost a fee.

Stocks and ETFs are always sold and bought separately, since they can't be exchanged.

  • Dollars: each exchange or sale is a fixed dollar amount.

Stocks & ETFs

How stock and ETF orders are sized. Mutual fund orders have their own setting under Mutual funds.

  • Dollars (recommended): sells and buys are dollar amounts. Sales bring back about the cash the plan expects, and buys invest every dollar. Needs fractional (dollar-based) trading, which Fidelity offers for most US stocks and ETFs.
  • Fractional shares: sells are a share count (to 0.001 share), so the cash you get back varies with the fill price. Buys are still in dollars. Also needs fractional trading.
  • Whole shares only: for accounts that can't trade fractional shares. Details below.

With dollars or fractional shares, a position being sold completely is sold as all shares, and large partial sales are always in shares: when a sale is 80% or more of a position, a dollar amount could exceed what the position is worth if the price drops before the order fills, so it switches to a share count, which can never exceed what you own.

Whole shares only

Only for accounts that can't trade fractional shares, for example an account where Fidelity's dollar-based (fractional) trading isn't enabled or isn't available. If your account can trade fractional shares, leave this off: dollar orders invest every dollar and land closer to target.

  • Sells are rounded to the nearest whole share. A sale of less than one share is skipped.
  • Positions being sold completely are sold as all shares, including any fraction you already hold. This assumes your broker lets you close out an existing fractional position even without fractional trading. If it won't, sell the whole shares and contact your broker about the fraction.
  • Buys are whole shares, each with a limit price. Place them as limit orders at that price. If the price climbs above it, the order simply won't fill instead of spending more than planned.
  • The plan adds up every buy at its limit price and makes sure the total can't exceed your cash, so market moves can't push you into a shortfall or, in a margin account, a margin loan.
  • Whole shares rarely add up exactly, so some cash is usually left over. The plan shows an estimate and warns you if it's more than 2% of the portfolio. Targets smaller than one share aren't bought.
  • Mutual fund orders aren't affected. Funds always trade in fractional shares at the closing price.

For the closest result, enter the actual cash in Cash available before final buys once the sells have filled. The buys are recalculated from the real number.

Protects against prices moving between planning and trading.

  • Buy limit prices are set this far above the current price, and every buy is sized so the total stays within your cash even if each one fills at its limit.
  • Sale proceeds are assumed to come in this much below current prices.

A bigger cushion is safer but leaves more cash unspent. About 0.5% to 1% suits widely traded ETFs; use more for volatile stocks or a fast-moving market. If a limit order doesn't fill because the price moved past it, download fresh prices and recalculate.

Orders

Placing them: tap any box to copy it, then paste it into Fidelity's trade ticket. Copied boxes get a check mark. The orders come in up to three steps:

  • Sell ETFs & stocks: on the stock and ETF ticket, choose Sell and set the amount to Dollars or Shares as shown. Place these first. All shares sells the whole position.
  • Mutual fund orders: on the mutual fund ticket. An Exchange sells one fund and buys another in a single order; a Sell goes to cash; a Buy uses cash. Place them before 4 p.m. Eastern (2 p.m. Mountain). They fill at that day's closing price, not right away.
  • Buy ETFs & stocks: once the sells have filled, choose Buy and set the amount to Dollars. If these buys depend on cash from a mutual fund sale, that cash only arrives after the close, so the plan tells you to place them the next business day.
  • ≈ amount: the estimated value at the prices entered.

Order type: Fidelity's dollar-based stock and ETF orders can be market or limit orders, good for the day only. For widely traded ETFs and large stocks a market order is usually fine; a limit order protects you from a bad fill on thinly traded ones.

Timing: trade stocks and ETFs during regular market hours, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. Eastern (7:30 a.m. to 2 p.m. Mountain). Avoid the first and last few minutes, when prices swing more. Place the sells and buys close together so prices don't drift much in between.

Settlement: stock, ETF, and most mutual fund trades settle in one business day. Brokers often let you buy with sale proceeds before they settle, but in a cash account, selling the new shares before the original sale settles can cause a good faith violation. Check your broker's rules for your account type. If a buy says the cash isn't available yet, wait until it is.

Fewest orders: each stock or ETF that's off target takes one order. Mutual funds that can be exchanged are paired up, so one exchange replaces a separate sell and buy wherever possible.

Taxes: in a taxable account, every sale and exchange can realize a capital gain or loss. Buying the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days of selling it at a loss can trigger the wash sale rule.

Placing them: enter the orders on Fidelity's mutual fund trade ticket. Tap any box to copy its value, then paste it into the matching field. Boxes show a check mark once copied so you can track where you are.

  • Exchange: on the ticket, choose the option to sell a mutual fund and use the proceeds to buy another. Paste the Sell fund, the Buy fund, and the share or dollar amount.
  • Buy: a plain purchase from the core cash position, in dollars.
  • Sell: sells into the core cash position. This only appears when you're keeping more cash than you have now.
  • All shares: the last order out of a fund you're dropping. If the ticket offers a sell-all-shares choice, use it; otherwise enter the share count shown, which is your whole position.
  • ≈ amount: the order's estimated value at the last close.

Timing: orders placed before 4 p.m. Eastern (2 p.m. Mountain) fill at that day's closing price; later ones fill the next business day. Both sides of an exchange are priced the same day.

Fewest orders: the tool groups funds whose dollar changes cancel out exactly and trades only within each group. A group of n funds needs n − 1 orders, so the plan uses the fewest orders possible. With more than 20 funds changing, it uses a faster method that comes close.

Before placing: check minimum first purchases, short-term redemption fees, and frequent-trading rules for each fund. In a taxable account, every exchange is a sale for tax purposes.
